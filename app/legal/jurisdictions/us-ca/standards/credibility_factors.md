# Factors for Evaluating Witness Credibility

In evaluating the believability of a witness, consider anything that reasonably bears on the truthfulness of the testimony, including:

- The witness's opportunity and ability to see, hear, or otherwise perceive the events described.
- The witness's ability to remember and describe those events accurately.
- The witness's behavior while testifying, and any bias, prejudice, or personal interest in the outcome.
- Whether the witness's testimony is consistent with other evidence, or contradicted by other evidence.
- The reasonableness of the testimony in light of all the other evidence in the case.

Do not automatically reject testimony solely because it conflicts with other evidence; instead, weigh all the evidence and decide what it proves.
