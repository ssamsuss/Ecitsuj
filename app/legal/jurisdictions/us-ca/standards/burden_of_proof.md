# Burden of Proof: Reasonable Doubt

The prosecution must prove every element of the charge beyond a reasonable doubt. Proof beyond a reasonable doubt is proof that leaves you with an abiding conviction that the charge is true. The evidence need not eliminate all possible doubt, because everything in life is open to some possible or imaginary doubt.

If, after weighing all the evidence, you have a reasonable doubt about whether the defendant is guilty, you must find the defendant not guilty. The defendant is never required to prove innocence or to produce any evidence at all.
